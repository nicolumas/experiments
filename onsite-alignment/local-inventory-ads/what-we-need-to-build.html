The decision

We build Option A

Google offers three setups for local inventory ads. Only one fits how LUMAS works.

A

Product pages with in-store availability

Store selector on the PDP. One national price from our feed. No store code in the URL.

Chosen
B

Store-specific pages with price and stock

Google puts a gallery code in the URL. Only pays off if prices differ per gallery. Ours don't.

Not needed
C

Pages without in-store availability

Google hosts the store info and audits our stock by hand. Slower, and we'd lose the page.

Rejected

Note

Option B costs per-gallery URLs and server-rendered store context above the fold, and buys nothing because LUMAS prices are per market, not per gallery.

What Google requires of the page

Five things the product page must do

All five apply on desktop, mobile and tablet. Failing any one of them fails the review.

1

Show the price we submitted

Same price as the feed, for the configuration shown.

2

Show gallery-level stock

As prominent as the buy-online option. Online stock is not gallery stock.

3

Name the gallery

City name is enough on the page. Full address when you open gallery details.

4

Answer in one interaction

One click or postcode. After that, stock and gallery both show, with no further clicks.

5

Be crawlable and stable

Google reads the page as first delivered. Price and stock must not change after load.

Note

Number 4 is the one most sites fail: gating the store check behind a size choice counts as two interactions.

What makes it work

Stock follows the configuration

The gallery holds one physical piece in one size and frame. Change the frame and the stock line must re-resolve with the price. If they can drift apart, the page is wrong.

Four states, no dead ends

In stock (3+ units) · Last one (1–2) · Not here, but at Düsseldorf · No gallery chosen. Add to cart stays live in every state.

Mobile: stock in the first screen

Cap the hero image so title, price and the gallery line are visible without scrolling.

Nothing may cover it

Cookie banner and overlays must never sit on top of price or availability.

Note

The configuration-to-stock binding is the architectural decision. Everything else is layout.

The feed

What we send Google

Gallery stock already exists in SAP. This is a mapping job, not a new data model.

Per gallery, per artwork

  • Gallery code: must match Google Business Profile
  • Article ID: must match our main product feed
  • Stock status
  • Quantity (optional, but we should send it)

How stock reads

  • 3 or more units → In stock
  • 1 or 2 units → Last one
  • 0 → not shown for that gallery
  • Roughly 8 in 10 pieces will read Last one

Keeping it current

  • A piece sells off the wall and the ad is stale
  • SAP refreshes daily, too slow on its own
  • First try: let Google read stock off our own page
  • Fallback: push updates from SAP as they happen

Note

If Google's own crawl keeps our stock current, the SAP integration drops out of scope entirely. Test that first.

Scope

Four galleries, but three separate setups

Local inventory ads are configured per country. This is the biggest surprise in the plan.

MarketGalleriesCatalogueSite
Germany Berlin Kudamm · Düsseldorf ~1.500 artworks lumas.de
Austria Vienna 575 artworks lumas.at
United States New York 1.100 artworks lumas.com

What that means

  • Three Merchant Center setups
  • Three feeds
  • Three page reviews, up to a week each
  • Germany and Austria need an Impressum
  • US prices in dollars, on a different site

Recommendation

Launch Germany first, copy to Austria, treat the US as its own track.

Only 204 artworks are stocked in all four galleries, so there is no shared set to build one campaign around.

Note

SAP codes 201101 and 201201 are Berlin 1 and Berlin 2; someone needs to confirm which is Kurfürstendamm.
