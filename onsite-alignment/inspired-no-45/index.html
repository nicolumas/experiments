Issue No. 45 · Autumn 2026 The Long Night An issue about pictures somebody waited for.

A word to begin with

Art can transform,
and it can connect.

This issue follows exactly that claim. We open the doors of studios, follow creative processes, give art rooms of its own and start conversations. Online and in our 19 galleries.

A particular focus falls again on our LUMAS Master editions, and on what LUMAS has stood for since 2004: curated art at gallery quality. Signed, limited editions that remain affordable. Extraordinary works with which you can enrich a world of your own.

Stefanie Harig and Marc Ullrich

Pigment, water and a table at three in the morning.

I · Interview with Marianna Gefen

By bike through Brooklyn, a stop at my favourite café between the apartment and the studio.

I prepare pigments and water, make the last adjustments. If the focus is missing it helps to put on music, or to play bass.

When the city goes quiet I shut myself in with water and colour, often until three or four in the morning. That solitude at night is essential to me.

That is when the real work begins.

III · Two hundred years of photography, in seven techniques

1826

The new question

What is an original in a world that produces billions of images every day and forgets them again at once?

Artificial intelligence makes pictures that look photographic without ever having seen light.

Again some are calling the end of the medium.

Again everything suggests the opposite is happening.

The easier pictures are to make, the more valuable what stands behind one becomes: an authorship, a position, a hand.

Andrés Galeano

What was documentation becomes memory.

Galeano rarely photographs. He collects, sorts and reorders pictures other people made and then forgot. His material comes from found estates and from archives: some 6,000 glass plates holding nothing but clouds, taken by Josep Pons i Girbau for an international cloud atlas. After Franco entered Barcelona in 1939 the service was closed and the archive seized.

Hundreds of skies side by side, not one of them exposed by him.

Andrés Galeano

Edition of 100, signed. Available in three sizes.

Niki Hare

The sentence stays the same. Only the handwriting is made of lines now.

Hare works in Tewkesbury on her word paintings: sentences that come out of her unfiltered, painted fast, because a feeling will not wait for the layers to dry.

This edition carries that into another material. A single continuous polyester thread, as tear-resistant as nylon, runs through the entire motif, strung between nails in a stainless Luma-titanium alloy.

Niki Hare · WHAT NOW

Edition of 100, signed. Available in three sizes.

Craig Alan · Populus

The idea came to him at a wedding. He saw the guests from above and realised that together they formed the shape of an eye.

From a distance, an icon.

Up close, hundreds of people, each one painted alone.

Axel Crieger

Stills from films that were never shot.

With Crieger, Audrey Hepburn has breakfast not at Tiffany's but at Katz's Delicatessen. Aristotle Onassis shrugs in front of a sunken yacht. He does not work with a camera but with what has already been photographed, building new pictures until the seams disappear.

Black and white, cinematic, and always a shade too perfect to have been true.

All works by Axel Crieger

IV · Daniel Kordan

A day spent hunting the midnight sun.

There are latitudes where the day does not end. The sun sinks towards the horizon, touches it and rises again. The light worth waiting for sits between eleven at night and three in the morning.

Four hours in which everything has to come together. The iceberg drifts on, the fog lifts, the colour turns.

VI · Formats

Boundless, or small enough to start with.

With edge lengths of up to 550 cm, the museum format fills a room. And at the other end, our Petites and framed Darlings bring the most loved works into a small format: limited editions in a passepartout and solid wood frame. The right choice for a collection that grows, or as a present.

VII · A visit to Miriam and Christian

It started with a mistake.

In 2014 the two of them are in the LUMAS gallery in Berlin looking for a housewarming present for a close friend. The date moves, once, then again, and by the time it is fixed the friend has bought a work himself.

So they unpacked it themselves. On trial, they said.

The trial has been running twelve years. They rehang about once a year, usually in winter, not because anything hangs wrong but because works show themselves differently in new company.

Some mistakes simply turn out to be right.

Anyone who collects over years ends up collecting themselves. Every work holds who you were when you chose it. From the story

VIII · Places where art lives

Anyone waiting for a flight here waits among the works.

In the new Terminal 3 at Frankfurt Airport there is not simply a gallery. The whole terminal has become one. Signed editions accompany travellers through the waiting areas, casually and at eye level. For many it is a first encounter with LUMAS, for some the beginning of a collection.
