What building this page turned up about the tools

  1. The Art Cloud has no address. It opens only through data-art-cloud-trigger in the menu, there is no URL. It cannot be linked, shared, bookmarked, put in a newsletter or indexed, and it carries hidden-sm hidden-xs, so on a phone it does not exist at all. Of the six tools it is the most elaborate and the only one with no page of its own.
  2. The room upload returns the same first ten works for every room. Three completely different rooms (a bright white living room, a warm taupe study, a bedroom) were sent to /upload-room/ on 27.08.2026 and produced a byte-identical first ten (ASH30, AFU07, MFS04, HHD08, CSM26, WDR09, MFS22, AZA04, MFS07, ALE12). Only from position 11 do the lists diverge: office∩bedroom 31 of 141, office∩living 40 of 124.
  3. The tail of those lists is good, and it is exactly what nobody sees. After the fixed head the tool visibly answers the room: the living room with two large windows returns Luc Dratwa's window series, the warm minimal study returns Adam Mørk's staircases and Wolfgang Uhlig's coastal minimalism, the bedroom returns portraits and fashion. This page deliberately shows the room-specific part, because otherwise the fixed head hides how good the tool is. Before launch, settle it: is that head a fallback, a boost, or a bug?
  4. The room upload promises a reason it does not deliver. The page says “our AI curator finds artworks that suit your style and explains why”. The response from /upload-room/ is {"products":[…]} with no explanation field of any kind. Either the reason gets built, or the promise comes out. The line under the results on this page is editorial, derived from the tags of the returned works, and is not generated by the service.
  5. The similarity search clusters on the source artist. With a catalogue work as the source, 12 of 12 hits for IME132 were the same artist, and 5 of 16 for SBN07. An uploaded image from outside has no source artist for it to hang on, which is why the source artist is removed here. For the PDP recommendation rail, which uses the same vectors, it stays a real problem.
  6. The Art Finder already hands its taxonomy to the browser. Every result tile carries data-product including categories_pairs, the work's eight curated coordinates. That is exactly what the ranking on this page runs on, with no API at all. Checked against live: 12 of 12, 9 of 12 and 11 of 12 of the local top-12 sat in the live top-12, and all of them in the live top-60. Two things follow: the sorting is reproducible, and the taxonomy is public.
  7. That taxonomy is the unused asset. Eight named axes, curated per work, across the catalogue. Outside the eight slider labels nobody names them anywhere: not on the PDP, not in collections, not in search, not in the newsletter. “Quiet, natural, minimal” is a collection that could be built from data that already exists.
  8. The art consultation is invisible on desktop. In the Artfinder dropdown the contact entry carries hidden-lg hidden-md and the Art Cloud carries hidden-sm hidden-xs. On a phone you therefore see the consultation but not the cloud; on desktop the cloud but not the consultation. The most expensive and most trust-building channel is missing on exactly the device with the longer sessions.
  9. The Art Finder stacks the same artist on the first screen. On the “calm and natural” preset, 12 tiles carry only 8 artists: three are Julia Christe, two Marja Pirilä, two Wolfgang Uhlig. Live behaves the same way and the demo reproduces it unchanged. A per-artist cap on the first page would widen the first impression a lot without touching the ranking.
  10. The feed's ratio field is not always right. In a sample of 14 works one was wrong (mir05: feed 3.27, actual 2.71). Anything that lays out from ratio alone will crop a work or leave a gap there. This page reserves the box from ratio and corrects it on load from naturalWidth, the same thing the shop's own product tiles already do.
  11. Prices diverge by market, not only over time. Every price on this page comes from the DE catalogue on 27.08.2026. The same edition sits at a different number elsewhere: Soo Burnell's “Marshall Street” is € 990 on lumas.de and € 799 on eu.lumas.com. That is why the English arm links to eu.lumas.com (English and euro) rather than uk.lumas.com (sterling), but prices have to be re-pulled per market before any launch.
  12. What is deliberately missing. No control arm, because there is no live page to reproduce. Prices are from 27.08.2026 and will drift. The ABlyft goals are unbound: the 16-goal library is not in the repo, so the marks are named in the source but not mapped. And the ledger was not read before this build.
