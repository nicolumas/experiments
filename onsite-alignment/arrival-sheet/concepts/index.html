Cookie banner (LM-14080) and country layer · Proposal · 17 Nov 2026

One arrival, not three interruptions

A visitor from France who lands on lumas.de answers the cookie question, gets a country pop-up two seconds later, switches, and is asked about cookies again on fr.lumas.com. Below: the restyle both layers need, then three ways to make them one continuous moment. The words stay as they are.

Today · phone · visitor in France

Three stops before the first artwork

Two layers built at different times, with different rules: one blocks the page, one doesn't; they don't share type, buttons or position. And because the cookie answer lives on one domain only, the visitor who follows the country advice is asked twice.

A · Styling and branding

One component, restyled to V2

Both layers become the same bottom sheet. Even if we never merge them, they stop looking like two products. Grey blocks are the page; the layer is drawn at real size and type.

Surface
White, one bone hairline on top and the single soft shadow V2 allows for utility surfaces. Square corners, like every V2 button.
Type
Title in Utile Display 22 px, sentence case. Body Archivo 14 px in text/secondary. Caps labels Archivo Bold 11 px.
Buttons
Primary in charcoal at its own width. The second action is the shop's underlined caps link with a 44 px tap target, never a second full-width bar.
Phone
Sits above the bottom nav, at most 45% of the screen, no backdrop. The page stays scrollable underneath.
Desktop
A floating 400 px card bottom right with 24 px inset, not a full-width bar. LiveChat stays bottom left.
Motion
Slides up in 240 ms. Changing step eases the height in 200 ms. Both off with reduced motion.
RecommendedB · Combine

Concept 1 · One sheet, two steps

Ask the country first, then cookies, inside the same sheet. Each question is asked once, and the cookie question is asked on the site the visitor ends up on, in its language.

Gains

  • Each question asked once. A switcher goes from three stops to two steps of one sheet.
  • Consent is given on the site that stores it, so no legal question about sharing it between domains.
  • No 2-second pop-in, no dark backdrop over the art.

Costs

  • Switching still reloads the page; the sheet reappears on arrival. Starting it at step 2 with a “LUMAS France ✓” line makes it read as a continuation.
  • One new line of copy per store for that arrival cue.

Build

consent-banner and geotargeting-popup become one web component. Twig already knows both answers on first paint (consent_state() and geotargeting), so it renders step 1, step 2 or both. The switch link adds ?from=de, which only tells the destination to open at step 2. No new service, no personal data in the URL.

Fewest tapsB · Combine

Concept 2 · One card, one tap

Country and cookies in one card. One tap on “OK und weiter” saves the cookie choice and opens the chosen site, which then doesn't ask again.

Gains

  • One tap for a visitor on the wrong site, nothing to answer on arrival.
  • A single surface, so it is the cleanest first impression.

Costs

  • The cookie choice made on lumas.de has to travel to fr.lumas.com. Needs legal sign-off and a new disclosure line.
  • One button doing two things sits close to bundling consent with something else; the country choice must stay clearly separate.
  • Tallest card: about 55% of a phone screen.
  • Cookie text shows in the language of the site the visitor is leaving.
Least intrusiveB · Combine

Concept 3 · Quiet hint

No country interruption at all. The cookie sheet carries one line pointing to the local site, and afterwards the suggestion waits as a small pill until closed.

Gains

  • One interruption for everyone; the art stays in view.
  • Smallest change to build.

Costs

  • Fewer visitors switch, so more of them reach checkout with the wrong prices, shipping and language.
  • Needs an A/B test on revenue per visitor from abroad before it replaces the pop-up.

Spotted while looking

Not changed in the wireframes

  • No way to decline on the first layer. German and French regulators expect declining to be as easy as accepting. Worth asking legal before the restyle ships; the layout has room for a second button.
  • “Hier bleiben” lasts 24 hours. The country_layer cookie expires after a day, so a French collector who prefers lumas.de is asked again tomorrow.
  • The pop-up shows the flag of the site you are on next to “not in your local shop”, which reads as a statement about you. The wireframes keep the flag on the switch button only.
  • “Shop” is on the Brandbook blacklist (“Galerie”). It is content, so it stays as it is here.
  • Caps tracking. Labels use 0.12em from Brandbook s04, not the 0.18em in the V2 Figma file.
