Navigation · addendum to the click dashboard

The same nav, re-ranked by value per visitor

The first dashboard ranked the header by clicks and click-weighted revenue. That measures demand, not conversion or the value a visitor carries when they use a destination. On a value lens, three low-click destinations turn out to be the most important surfaces LUMAS has, and one item we cut is doing more work than its clicks let on.

Scope: lumas.de. Revenue and order facts from DWH (last 12 months, canonical). On-site behaviour from GA4 (last 90 days, directional). Read alongside the click dashboard and the strategy report.

The reframe

Clicks told us what people touch. Value tells us what pays the rent.

Both are true. A funnel that only optimises for clicks quietly demotes the destinations that carry the money, because the highest-value channels are, by nature, lower in volume.

What clicks said

Product browsing dominates; galleries, advisory and B2B look marginal

Ranked by volume, the physical galleries, art consultation and B2B sit near the bottom, and the Art Finder reads as a niche tool worth cutting.

Home + search + product pages take the top of every click chart.
What value says

The gallery network out-earns the entire online shop; B2B is the highest-AOV visitor there is

Ranked by revenue and value per visitor, galleries, editions, advisory and B2B are the assets the nav should be protecting and surfacing, not trimming.

The one to watch: the Art Finder

It is the clearest "clicks are not value" case in the whole exercise, and it landed on our own edit. On click volume the Art Finder looks trimmable; on engagement it behaves like the homepage, on 14k sessions a quarter and 210 seconds a visit. We have re-nested it as a featured gateway inside Inspiration. If it is ever removed, that should be a measured A/B decision, not an inference from a click chart.

04

Two additions the value lens justifies

You asked about testing new items like Art Consultation and B2B. Both are justified by value, not by existing clicks, and both are effectively invisible online today: a regex sweep of lumas.de found no trafficked front door for beratung, b2b, business, firmenkunden or trade.

Business / B2B Add

B2B is €2.15M a year at €1,529 AOV, 2.3× the B2C order. Yet it has no discoverable online entry point. A €2.15M segment with no front door is a gap, not a low-priority item. A dedicated B2B landing, surfaced in the nav or the top utility bar, gives that visitor somewhere to land.

Evidence: B2B €2.15M / 1,407 orders / €1,529 AOV vs B2C €663 [DWH · LTM · high]. Zero trafficked b2b/business/trade path [GA4 · 90d · high].

Art Consultation Test

No consultation page draws traffic today, but the channel it feeds is the €13.2M gallery book, at premium AOVs (Zurich €1,470, New York €1,235, Miami €857 per order). A nav door that routes online browsers to book a consultation or gallery appointment is a bridge to the highest-value channel LUMAS has. The "Beratungstermin buchen" button already in Galerien is the seed.

Evidence: Gallery revenue €13.2M, premium per-order values [DWH · LTM · high]. Both now prototyped in a "Beratung" nav item housing private advisory + B2B.
