Seven ideas for the messy middle, on the live shop

Each idea comes from Google's Decoding Decisions paper and our own buyer paths (712 orders, 1 to 24 Nov), and sits on today's lumas.de pages with production's fonts, buttons, prices and product cards. The site reads which mode a visitor is in and shows each idea only where it fits.

Pages are captured from the live shop on first visit; nothing reaches the real shop (the cart is simulated). Add ?mm=none to any page in this copy to see production without the ideas, ?mm=all to bring them back.

Idea 3 · A site that reads the mode

Step into a visitor's shoes

Each link puts you into one mode with matching history, then the site's own signals take over: works seen in an earlier visit make a returning visitor, an add to cart makes an evaluator, a cart or checkout landing a decided one.

ExploringFirst visit from an ad. Lands on an artist page: what collectors choose comes first; no scarcity claims. Coming backViewed five works on earlier days. Homepage, header and listings lead back to them. EvaluatingLegendary is in the cart. On another work: the cart bar, side by side at true size. DecidedKnows what to buy. Nothing extra on the way: no welcome, no strip, no bar.

Every mm_* event carries mm_mode, so GA4 could report revenue per visitor within each mode, against a holdout that sees today's site.

Idea 1 · Coming back · homepage, header, listings

Your works, everywhere

A returning visitor's homepage opens on the work they configured in an earlier visit: their size, frame and today's price, one tap to the cart. On desktop a header icon next to the wish list counts their recent works and opens them in a panel; on listings a slim strip above the grid does the same on every device. A work opened from any of these arrives in the size and frame they chose.

Why: 51% of tracked revenue comes from buyers who came back for a work they had already seen. Returning visitors who land on the homepage re-find their works only 22% of the time, on listings 15%.

HomepageListing with strip

Measures: mm_return_view, mm_works_open, mm_works_click, mm_works_restore; primary: re-find rate, then revenue per returning visitor. In production the shop's own last-seen cookie (1 year, 20 works) feeds this.

Idea 2 · Evaluating · every page after the first add to cart

Cart stays in sight

After the first add to cart, a compact bar keeps the work, its size and price and "Zur Kasse" in view. On another work's product page it offers "Vergleichen": both works side by side, drawn at their true relative size, with "Beide nehmen" or checkout.

Why: 34% of buyers view another work between first add to cart and paying; 15% add a second. Returning buyers who land straight in the cart place the highest-value orders (€1,023 average).

Product page with the bar

Measures: mm_cartbar_view, mm_cartbar_click, mm_compare_open; primary: checkout starts from non-cart pages and second-work orders. Built to follow production's cart layer, never to repeat it.

Idea 4 · Exploring · search and listings

Art's rules of thumb

Type a colour, format or size into search ("schwarz weiß new york", "panorama meer", "xxl") and the dropdown first offers the shop's own filter, keeping the rest of the query. Listing cards gain one quiet line: the work's shape and how many sizes it comes in.

Why: site search, 90 days: schwarz-weiß about 400 sessions, panorama, Quer- and Hochformat, XXL and klein; "signiert" only 13. The filters (colors, dimension, width) already exist. Rooms (Küche, Bad) need room pages first.

Measures: mm_thumb_view, mm_thumb_click; primary: search exits and clicks from search to an artwork page.

Idea 5 · Exploring · artist pages

Proof where explorers land

The artist page opens on the four works collectors chose most in the last 30 days, as production's own product cards, above the biography. Isabelle Menin: Midnight 01, From a Dream 04, Only In Your Heart 03, Dew (DWH, 26 Oct to 24 Nov, by revenue across all sizes and channels). Production's live Social Proof Bar already carries viewers on the product page.

Idea 6 · Evaluating · product pages

Scarcity only when true

The demand indicator keeps its price ladder but says only what always holds: the edition's price rises in steps as it sells. No sell-out claim, no copy count, no "!". Visitors still exploring do not see it at all. Luc Dratwa's Legendary carries today's strongest claim ("Hohe Nachfrage – Bestseller bald ausverkauft!").

Why: the indicator is live at 100% and was never tested; 501 sizes say "bald ausverkauft" with a median of 15 years left to sell out. The paper found scarcity the weakest of six biases, and off-putting to explorers.

Measures: mm_demand_view with arm; primary: revenue per product-page visitor. About 7 weeks for ±20% at current DE traffic.

Idea 7 · Evaluating · campaign

Free instead of % off

During Black Week, arm B gives the frame instead of 20%: the visitor pays the unframed price for a framed work. Adding an unframed acrylic to the cart first offers the frames, in the gift dialog style the cart already uses. Every number is production's: Rive Gauche 75 × 100 cm is € 999 unframed, € 1,299 in the Basel frame, so the frame is worth € 300 against today's € 200 advantage.

Why: the power of free was a top-2 bias in 18 of 31 categories. Art Week rules carried 68% of 2026 online growth and discount depth rose from 14.9% to 16.7%. Equal cost per order needs finance: a frame's cost is not its retail value.
