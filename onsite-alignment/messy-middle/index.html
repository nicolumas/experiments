Seven ideas for the messy middle, on the live shop

Each idea comes from Google's Decoding Decisions paper and our own buyer paths (712 orders, 1 to 24 Nov), and sits on today's lumas.de pages with production's fonts, buttons, prices and product cards. For every idea, the first phone shows the page as it is today and the next ones show the same spot with the idea.

Dashed outline: the part of the page that changes.

Pages are captured from the live shop on first visit; nothing reaches the real shop (the cart is simulated). Add ?mm=none to any page in this copy to see production without the ideas, ?mm=all to bring them back.

Idea 1 · Coming back · homepage, header, listings

Your works, everywhere

Red-team: build with changes

A visitor who looked at works on an earlier day finds them again wherever they land, in the size and frame they chose.

What changes

  1. Homepage stage (2). "Hier warst du stehen geblieben" replaces the campaign stage for returning visitors only. New visitors see today's homepage.
  2. Listing strip (3). Above the product grid on every listing, for returning visitors. Tapping it opens the sheet (4).
  3. Header icon (desktop only). A clock icon next to the wish list, with a count. Hidden on phones, where it would collide with the logo.
  4. Same configuration. Any work opened from these arrives in the size and frame chosen last time, not the default.

Why: 51% of tracked revenue comes from buyers who came back for a work they had already seen. Returning visitors who land on the homepage re-find their works only 22% of the time, on listings 15%.

Red-team: add a way back on phone artwork pages (54% of returning visits land there). Re-find rate moves in under a week; revenue per returning visitor needs about 16 weeks for ±20%.

Events: mm_return_view, mm_works_open, mm_works_click, mm_works_restore. In production the shop's own last-seen cookie (1 year, 20 works) feeds this.

Idea 6 · Evaluating · product pages

Scarcity only when true

Red-team: build with changes

The demand indicator under the price keeps its price ladder but only says what always holds. Luc Dratwa's Legendary, which carries today's strongest claim.

What changes

  1. Headline. "Bald ausverkauft!" becomes "Der Preis dieser Edition steigt in Stufen, je mehr Exemplare verkauft sind." New editions say they start at the opening price; the top tier says "letzte Preisstufe".
  2. No copy count, no "!". The number of copies left in a price stage reads like copies left in the edition, so it goes.
  3. Hidden for explorers (3). Visitors on a first look don't see the indicator; the social proof bar's "Preis steigt" count is hidden too.
  4. Arm C (not shown) removes the indicator for everyone, as a holdback.

Why: the indicator is live at 100% and was never tested; 501 sizes say "bald ausverkauft" with a median of 15 years left to sell out. The paper found scarcity the weakest of six biases, and off-putting to explorers.

Red-team: run as a non-inferiority test with a holdback, and get a legal check of today's wording first. About 10 weeks for ±20% revenue per product-page visitor.

Events: mm_demand_view with arm. Links: arm C.

Idea 5 · Exploring · artist pages

Proof where explorers land

Red-team: test the ad destination first

The artist page opens on the four works collectors chose most in the last 30 days, as production's own product cards, above the biography.

What changes

  1. Four works above the bio. Production's product cards, ranked by DWH revenue across sizes and channels, 26 Oct to 24 Nov.
  2. Honest subline. "Ausgewählt nach den Verkäufen der letzten 30 Tage, online und in unseren Galerien." Ranked by revenue, so it never says "am häufigsten".
  3. Link to all works. The full list stays where it is; one link jumps there.

Why: Paid Social sends about 30,000 visits to artist pages at €0.08 per visit, against €0.67 on artwork-page landings. A rolling 30-day top 25 sold about 10× more per work than "Letzte Exemplare" in 17 of 17 campaign windows.

Red-team: a site test would need about 850 weeks on revenue. Test the ad destination first (artist page vs a work page), on artist-to-artwork clicks.

Events: mm_proof_view, mm_proof_click.

Idea 2 · Evaluating · every page after the first add to cart

Cart stays in sight

Red-team: do not build yet

After the first add to cart, a slim bar docked on the bottom navigation keeps the work and the way to checkout in view. Legendary is in the cart; the visitor is looking at From a Dream 04.

What changes

  1. The bar (2). Shop's bone colour, hairline on top, flush on the bottom navigation; it slides away while scrolling down and returns on scroll up. On desktop it is a small card bottom right.
  2. Compare (3). Only on another work's page. Draws both at their real outer size, so "which is bigger on my wall" needs no maths.
  3. Never twice. Production's cart layer still opens on add to cart; the bar follows it, it does not repeat it.

Why: 34% of buyers view another work between first add to cart and paying; 15% add a second.

Red-team: the Cart Layer test (39094149) cut checkout reach by 11%, and the header cart already exists. Hold until that result is understood.

Events: mm_cartbar_view, mm_cartbar_click, mm_compare_open.

Idea 3 · A site that reads the mode

Step into a visitor's shoes

Red-team: kill as a feature, keep as targeting

Not a screen of its own: it decides which of the other ideas a visitor sees. Each link puts you into one mode with matching history, then the site's own signals take over.

ExploringFirst visit from an ad. Artist page leads with what collectors choose; no scarcity claims. Coming backViewed five works on earlier days. Homepage, header and listings lead back to them. EvaluatingLegendary is in the cart. On another work: the cart bar, side by side at true size. DecidedKnows what to buy. Nothing extra on the way: no welcome, no strip, no bar.

How a mode is set

  1. Decided: lands on cart, checkout, account or order tracking.
  2. Evaluating: something is in the cart.
  3. Coming back: viewed works in an earlier visit.
  4. Exploring: everyone else.

Red-team: as a feature it has no outcome of its own to test. Keep the rules as audiences in ABlyft for ideas 1 and 6.

Every mm_* event carries mm_mode.

Idea 4 · Exploring · search and listings

Art's rules of thumb

Red-team: kill, salvage room pages

Type a colour, format or size into search and the suggestions first offer the shop's own filter, keeping the rest of the query. Here: "schwarz weiß" in the phone search.

What changes

  1. Filter suggestion (2). Colour (colors=), format (dimension=) and size (width=) words become the real filter; other words stay in the query.
  2. Card line (3). Shape and size count under the price.

Why: site search, 90 days: schwarz-weiß about 400 sessions, then panorama, Quer- and Hochformat, XXL and klein.

Red-team: filter-word searches are about 3% of the 4.3% of sessions that search; 47 weeks to read. Room words (Küche, Bad) point to room pages instead.

Idea 7 · Evaluating · campaign

Free instead of % off

Red-team: kill as designed

During Black Week, arm B gives the frame instead of 20%: the unframed price for a framed work. Axel Crieger, Rive Gauche, 75 × 100 cm.

What changes

  1. Price block (2). Shows the unframed price for the framed configuration instead of a struck-through price.
  2. Frame offer (3). Choosing an unframed acrylic opens the frames before the cart.

Why: the power of free was a top-2 bias in 18 of 31 categories in the paper.

Red-team: 74% of online buyers take no frame, so arm B gives most of them nothing. Salvage: a frame-attach test outside campaigns.
