Where we start

The material, honestly

The brief said there is no artist content. That is half true. There is no media, but the magazine page is finished, approved editorial prose, and it is the strongest writing anyone will produce about this work before October.

We have

  • Seventeen works, every image verified and hotlinkable at full resolution.
  • The magazine spread: Hollywood given a second reality, Hepburn at Katz's rather than Tiffany's, the "grammar of glamour", Warhol, Interview, Beuys.
  • A few room renders from the design team.
  • Real prices and three formats per work.

We do not have

  • Any photograph of the artist.
  • Studio or process material, and no film.
  • A single word of artwork description in the feed. Every record is empty.
  • Anything the artist has said in his own voice.

The decision

Three directions

Same works, same toolkit, same date. What changes is the organising idea, and how much writing each one needs before the first of the month.

Chosen

Photographs that never happened

The page presents itself as an archive of images that look recovered and cannot exist. Each caption states the impossible fact plainly: who, where, and the year it could not have been. The artist arrives late, as the answer to the question the page has been asking.

Turns the missing artist content into the mechanism rather than an apology. Work that claims to be found is better served by an absent author than by a studio portrait we do not have. It is also the only direction where the reveal is the sales argument: you wanted it to be real, it is not, you can own it.

Folded in

The grammar of glamour

Entertainment Weekly's phrase becomes the structure. Crieger reuses a fixed vocabulary, and each chapter teaches one term of it before the works arrive.

Not taken whole, because a taxonomy explains the work well and moves the reader very little. Its chapter logic survives as the three movements inside the chosen direction.

Not taken

One picture, told properly

The whole top of the page spent on a single work, read slowly, the way a gallerist would walk you through it. Then the collection opens out behind it.

Least copy, highest bar on that copy. Ruled out because a work is never cropped here, so a long essay cannot cut into detail, and the remaining sixteen become an afterthought grid.

The page

Three movements

The grammar, cast. Fifteen works carry the story, each one captioned with the fact that cannot be true. The remaining two are dealt with below.

Movement I

The icon

A face you know, standing somewhere she never stood. The caption does the work, and the page has not yet said who made any of this.

Movement II

The machine

The vehicle as co-star. Yacht, car, jet, crop duster. Speed is the glamour and the human is almost incidental. This is the movement that earns the charcoal band, and the CMS gives it to us without a single custom rule.

Movement III

The stage

Architecture as the set, the figure placed on it like a performer. The shortest movement, and the one that hands over to the reveal.

After this, the turn: every one of these was built. The magazine paragraph lands here almost verbatim, and it needs no portrait, because the absence has been the device all along. Then the editions, all formats, real prices.

Open

Two works cause problems

Both need a decision before the page is written, and neither is a design question.

Imagen, 2016

Sixteen works are sepia and silver. This one is saturated blue and red, and there is no Hollywood in it at all. Dropped into any movement it breaks the spell the page spends five screens building.

  • Leave it out of the story. It still appears in the closing edition grid, where a break in tone reads as range rather than error.
  • Give it the last word.Proposed Placed alone after the reveal, as the proof that the sepia was a choice and not a habit. Colour arriving late becomes the page's final turn.
  • Put it in The stage. Defensible, since it is a constructed tableau against a wall of frames, but a colour shift mid-page will read as a mistake.

Rive Gauche, Grace Kelly, 2012

Near identical to Rive Gauche (2013), which is already in Movement I. It is also the only discontinued work in the set, so it cannot be sold on 1 October.

Proposed: leave it off the page entirely. A work nobody can buy, standing next to its own twin, costs more than it gives.

Before writing

Four questions

Everything else is settled. These four change the copy, so they come first.

  • Imagen

    Out of the story, last word after the reveal, or inside The stage.

  • Rive Gauche, Grace Kelly

    Confirm it comes off the page, or say why it stays.

  • German address form

    The brand book puts the magazine's editorial voice in Sie and the web in du, and this page sits between them. The two are never mixed in one asset. The narrative sections can avoid direct address entirely, which leaves the question live only at the close.

  • Locales on 1 October

    Assumed DE, EN and FR. Copy is written inline per locale in the CMS, so each language is separate work rather than a key swap.

Concept board, not a prototype. The page itself is built next, as a CMS fragment. Artwork images load live from the LUMAS catalogue and are shown whole, at true ratio, never cropped and never dimmed.
