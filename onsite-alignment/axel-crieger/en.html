Axel Crieger

Beyond the scenes

The archive of what never happened

Deleted scenes, but different: Axel Crieger's art moves between what was and what might have been. His material sits between found object and invention, between photography, drawing and digital editing, composed so precisely that Hollywood gains a second reality in his work.

The icon

Familiar faces, impossible places

Audrey Hepburn doesn't have breakfast at Tiffany's. She has it at Katz's Delicatessen. Marilyn Monroe, Steve McQueen and Brigitte Bardot appear among New York's street canyons, yachts and private jets, in scenes that never happened. They are so convincing that his fictions look like lost footage from a more glamorous past.

The machine

Speed, standing perfectly still

Yacht, car, aircraft. The machine is the star, and the human sits perfectly still in the middle of it. The German artist blends photography, collage and digital post-production so seamlessly that reality turns into fantasy and icons into legends. Entertainment Weekly aptly called his visual language the “Grammar of Glamour.”

The stage

The second reality

Crieger moved early on in the very cultural cosmos his works now reference: he met Andy Warhol, was published in Warhol's magazine Interview, and earned recognition from Joseph Beuys. He begins where myth and reality have long since blurred, where the collective image overrides mere biography. It is in this in-between space that his fiction becomes a second reality.
